# BerryCheck — Sales Pitch

## The One-Liner

"We keep the receipt so you don't eat the rejection."

---

## The Pitch (Shed-Floor Version)

You ever had a DC reject a load and you know damn well it was good when it left? What'd you do? You ate it. Because you had nothing to show them.

What if every pallet that left your dock had a QC record on it — timestamped, graded, photos — and when the DC calls, you pull it up in 10 seconds and say "here's what it looked like when it left here, let's talk about what happened on your end."

That's what this does. Your guy pulls a clamshell, snaps it, the system grades it, and now you've got a record. Three samples per pallet, same SOP every time, and it's all logged.

You're not changing how you work. You're just keeping the receipt.

---

## Why It Lands

- **Sell the pain, not the tech.** Every operator has a scar from a bullshit rejection. You're selling the thing that makes sure it doesn't happen again.
- **No new workflow.** They already pull clamshells. They already eyeball the fruit. Same process, now there's a record.
- **Defense, not offense.** You're not promising better fruit. You're promising proof when someone says it was bad.
- **Everyone understands a receipt.** Not one word about software, data layers, or AI. Just: here's what left your dock, timestamped, graded, photographed.

---

## The Upsell (After They're Running)

"Hey, you know that grower — the one on route 84? His Star variety has been trending soft across the last three weeks. Not failing yet, but the headroom's shrinking. You might want to run his next load first thing in the morning before it sits in the heat."

Now you're not just the receipt. You're the early warning system. That's when they can't live without you.

Don't lead with this. Lead with the rejected pallet. Every single time.

---

## The Grower Angle (Shed-as-Consultant)

The DC data is garbage for grower feedback. It's not robust, it's biased by market conditions, and it's suspect when things go sideways. Your data is captured at the source — before transit, before cold chain breaks, before the DC's mood enters the picture. It's the cleanest read on that grower's actual fruit quality that exists anywhere in the chain.

**The pitch to the shed operator:**

"Your growers are going to ask you why their fruit got rejected. Right now you shrug because you don't have the data either. What if you could show them exactly how their fruit graded every week of the season — by variety, by block, by pick crew? Now you're not the middleman delivering bad news. You're the guy with the answers. That's how you keep your best growers."

**What it gives the grower:**

- Weekly trend data by variety and block — not gut feelings, real numbers
- The ability to correlate: "My Star ran at 80 lugs/hr with 3% blowoff. My Emerald ran 120 lugs/hr with 0.5%." Now planting decisions are driven by what survives the shed and the DC, not just what yields in the field.
- A reason to stay with you over the shed down the road that can't tell them anything

**The transparency play (opt-in):**

Tie the QC report to the operations calculator. Show the grower their fruit's full lifecycle under your roof:
- QC grade at receiving
- Line speed and throughput when packed
- Blowoff rate (sorter rejects) and reasons

Most sheds treat line speed and ops data like trade secrets. The shed willing to share it is saying: "We're not hiding anything. Here's exactly what happened to your fruit." That takes guts — but it's a competitive advantage disguised as honesty. Build it as a toggle. QC data is the default. Ops data is the power move for sheds that want to differentiate.

---

## What You're Actually Building (Don't Say This Out Loud)

The app is the trojan horse. The real business is the structured quality data layer between the shed and the buyer — the digital record of every handshake in the supply chain that currently lives on clipboards and trust.

Every shed that plugs in captures data that makes the whole network smarter:
- Grower performance trends across varieties, blocks, weeks
- DC rejection patterns correlated to real QC records
- Predictive flags before fruit fails — not after

The shed operator thinks he bought a CYA tool for DC disputes. What he actually did was plug into a data network. He doesn't need to know that yet.

## Objection Handling

**"We already check our fruit."**
I know you do. This doesn't change that. It just means when the DC says otherwise, you've got the timestamp and the photo to back it up.

**"My guys are too busy for this."**
It's one phone snap per sample. Three samples per pallet. Takes less time than filling out the clipboard you're already using.

**"How much does it cost?"**
Less than one rejected pallet. How many did you eat last season?

**"The big sheds use [Highland / HarvestGear / etc]."**
Those systems grade fruit on the belt. This grades the decision to ship. Different problem. They tell you what the sorter saw. This tells you what left your dock. You need both — but they don't sell this part.
